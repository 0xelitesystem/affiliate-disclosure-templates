# affiliate-disclosure-templates

Plain-English, FTC-compliant disclosure language for video, blog, social, podcast, and email. Copy what you need.

## Why this exists

The FTC's Endorsement Guides require clear, conspicuous disclosure when you have a material connection to something you recommend. "Material connection" includes: affiliate links, free products, paid sponsorships, family/employer relationships. Vague disclosures (`#sp`, `#partner`, `link in bio`) do not meet the bar.

Most creators want to comply but don't want to write 5 lawyer-vetted sentences from scratch. This repo is the templates. Pick one, paste it, customize the brand name.

## Important

- This is **not legal advice**. The templates follow FTC guidance as published, but enforcement actions and rules evolve. Read the [FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) at least once a year.
- Different countries have different rules (UK ASA, EU directives, Canada CASL, Australia ACCC). Templates here are written for **US (FTC)** unless marked otherwise.
- This repo covers DISCLOSURE language only. Tax, contract, and IP issues around affiliate/sponsored content are out of scope.

## Disclosure types covered

| Type | When to use |
|---|---|
| Affiliate link | You earn commission when someone buys via your link |
| Sponsored / paid promotion | Brand paid you to talk about them |
| Gifted product | You received the product free, no payment, no posting requirement |
| Sample for review | You received the product free, ongoing review relationship |
| Equity / ownership | You own equity, are an advisor, or work for the company |
| Family / employer | Personal relationship affecting the recommendation |
| Beta / preview access | You got something before public release |

## What makes a disclosure compliant

The FTC consistently emphasizes:

1. **Clear**, written in plain language a typical viewer/reader will understand
2. **Conspicuous**, placed where people will notice it BEFORE engaging with the recommendation
3. **Unavoidable**, not buried in long descriptions, not behind a "more" link
4. **Up front**, at or near the start of the relevant content, not at the end
5. **Specific**, name the relationship type ("paid sponsorship", "affiliate link"), not just `#ad` (though `#ad` is acceptable when nothing else fits the medium)

Hashtags like `#sponsored` or `#ad` are acceptable on social posts when they are clearly visible, not buried in a hashtag wall, and used in addition to the actual disclosure when space allows.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Putting affiliate disclosure ONLY in your channel's "About" page
- Disclosing only in the description box when the recommendation is in the video
- Using `#sp`, `#partner`, `#collab`, `#thanks_to` (none of these meet the FTC's clarity bar)
- Disclosing AFTER the call-to-action ("buy now! ... oh by the way I get a commission")
- Calling sponsored content a "review" without disclosing the payment
- Hiding disclosure inside a long block of hashtags
